# OpenEnergyOS

**Where energy transition becomes a living specification.**

OpenEnergyOS is a modular, AGPL-3.0+ licensed specification-driven platform for designing, simulating, optimizing, and governing large-scale energy transition systems. It is built for municipalities, states, and national governments that need to model and execute the shift away from oil dependency using transparent, auditable, physics-based systems.

The platform treats specifications as the core executable layer. AI systems generate simulations, infrastructure plans, policy scenarios, and implementation roadmaps directly from these specifications while humans define intent, constraints, and governance.

---

# Key Features

## 1. Spec-Driven Energy System Design
- Energy systems defined entirely through structured specifications
- AI generates simulations, models, and deployment plans from specs
- Version-controlled infrastructure logic
- Reproducible policy and engineering outcomes

## 2. Whole-System Energy Simulation
- Electricity, transport, heating, and industry modeled as a single system
- Multi-decade scenario forecasting
- Hourly and seasonal load simulation
- Cross-sector coupling (grid, fuel, heat, mobility)

## 3. Oil Dependency Modeling
- Identification of petroleum usage across all sectors
- Transportation, aviation, marine, agriculture, and industrial fuel tracking
- Replacement pathway modeling for every dependency
- Oil phase-out trajectory simulation

## 4. Infrastructure Digital Twin
- Real-time modeled replica of energy infrastructure
- Power plants, grids, pipelines, storage, and distributed assets
- Asset-level dependency mapping
- System state simulation and forecasting

## 5. Cost and Economic Optimization
- Lifecycle cost modeling (CAPEX, OPEX)
- Levelized cost of energy calculations
- Infrastructure ROI comparison
- Public budget impact modeling
- Cost per emissions reduced analysis

## 6. Grid Optimization Engine
- Load balancing and demand response simulation
- Storage dispatch optimization
- Peak demand reduction modeling
- Grid reliability and congestion analysis

## 7. Distributed Energy Coordination
- Solar, wind, storage, and microgrid integration
- Vehicle-to-grid coordination
- Local energy market modeling
- Decentralized energy balancing

## 8. Waste Energy Recovery Systems
- Industrial heat reuse modeling
- Data center thermal energy capture
- District heating integration
- Regenerative energy system design

## 9. Physics-Based Simulation Engine
- Thermodynamics modeling
- Electrical load and grid physics
- Mechanical and fluid systems simulation
- Energy conversion efficiency constraints

## 10. Municipal Energy Planning Suite
- City-level infrastructure modeling
- Public building energy optimization
- Fleet electrification planning
- Water and waste system energy modeling

## 11. State-Level Energy Coordination
- Regional grid balancing
- Utility coordination models
- Economic incentive simulation
- Workforce transition modeling

## 12. National Energy Planning System
- Energy independence modeling
- Strategic infrastructure planning
- Import reduction pathways
- National security energy analysis

## 13. Infrastructure Transparency Layer
- Ownership mapping of energy assets
- Energy flow visualization
- Public accountability dashboards
- Funding and control transparency

## 14. AI Optimization Engine
- Infrastructure design optimization
- Policy scenario comparison
- Investment planning models
- Failure prediction and mitigation

## 15. Specification Management System
- Version-controlled system specifications
- Regulatory and engineering constraint tracking
- API contracts for all modules
- Simulation definitions as executable artifacts

## 16. Legal & Regulatory Alignment Engine
- Statute and regulation parsing from official sources
- Court ruling and case law analysis using government records
- Detection of conflicts between infrastructure plans and law
- Compliance validation against primary legal sources only
- Reform recommendation engine for lawful transition pathways

## 17. Public Engagement Layer
- Transparent dashboards for citizens
- Scenario comparison tools
- Participatory planning interfaces
- Public infrastructure visualization

# Architecture

OpenEnergyOS is fully modular. Each system functions independently but communicates through a shared specification layer.

Modules can be deployed individually or combined into full-stack national infrastructure systems.
